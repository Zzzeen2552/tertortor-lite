## 0. People and places (read first)

The channel is about Indonesians. Make faces, clothes, streets and landscape unmistakably Indonesian and tropical in every prompt.

**Heritage in the subject sentence.** Write "a Javanese boy", "a Minang trader", "a Peranakan Chinese-Indonesian shopkeeper". See heritage anchors in `style-bible.md`.

**Regional dress (simplified, era-appropriate):**
- Javanese: batik sarong or kain, beskap or surjan jackets, blangkon headcloth; women in kebaya and batik wraps with a bun.
- Sundanese: loose black pangsi outfit, iket head cloth for men; kebaya and sarong for women.
- Betawi: sadariah (loose white shirt and sarong), peci.
- Minang: sarong, black peci or saluak headcloth, long shirt; women in baju kurung and a draped selendang.
- Bugis and Makassar: silk sarong, songkok hat; women in baju bodo.
- Batak: ulos shoulder cloth.
- Madurese: striped shirt, loose black trousers, sarong, often with a headcloth.
- Everyday laborers anywhere: short trousers or rolled sarong, singlet or loose shirt, bare feet, bamboo hat (caping).

**Chinese-Indonesian (Tionghoa) settings and dress:**
- Pecinan streets with narrow two-story shophouses (ruko), carved wooden shutters, red paper lanterns, ancestral altar glimpsed inside, tea and abacus on the counter, rice, spices and textiles stacked by the wall.
- A temple (kelenteng) with red columns, upswept roof ridges and incense, in a tropical courtyard.
- Men: loose white or dark cotton jackets with cloth-knot buttons, trousers, sometimes a skullcap or a Western-style shirt and vest; women: kebaya encim with embroidered edges and a batik sarong, hair in a bun.
- Placed among Indonesian neighbors, vendors and customers; Chinese-Indonesian families speak and trade within the local community.
- Use warm, individual, dignified expressions and details; never exaggerated features or stereotyped gestures.

**Mixed crowds.** Port cities and markets blend Javanese, Sundanese, Betawi, Madurese, Minang, Bugis, Chinese-Indonesian, Arab-Indonesian and Indo-European people. Show variety in clothing and complexion within a plausible, non-caricatured mix.

**Place anchors (choose one):**
- **Batavia / Jakarta (Kota Tua):** canals, white colonial warehouses, Chinatown (Glodok) shophouses, harbor at Sunda Kelapa with pinisi schooners.
- **Surabaya:** busy Tanjung Perak harbor, red-brick merchant houses, Arab quarter, Chinese shophouses, hot haze.
- **Semarang:** old-town (Kota Lama) with a Dutch church, canal and Chinatown, damp stained walls.
- **Medan:** wide verandas, tropical Dutch-style company buildings, plantation trucks.
- **Makassar:** old fort, Bugis phinisi ships at the quay, silk sarongs.
- **Bandung / Priangan:** cooler, Art Deco villas, green hills.
- **Padang:** Minang rumah gadang roofs, trading wharf.
- **Javanese village or small town:** joglo roofs, rice paddies, river with bamboo rafts, warung, pasar with banana leaf wrapping.

**Tropical cues (add at least two):** coconut palms, banana plants, flamboyant (red-blooming) trees, rain trees, frangipani, bamboo, rice fields, volcano silhouettes (Java), humid haze, monsoon clouds, mud and puddles, terracotta tile roofs with deep eaves, shuttered verandas, whitewashed walls with green-brown stains, bicycles, becak, delman, warung stalls.

**Avoid:** European village streets without tropical elements, bare temperate trees or snow, generic East Asian (Chinese or Japanese) city looks, temples or lanterns that read as in China rather than in Indonesia, Western faces for everyone.

## 1. Late colonial period

- **Streets:** cobblestone and macadam, tram or railway tracks at the edge, gas or early electric street lamps, horse carts (delman/sado), early bicycles, 1930s sedans and trucks, becak in later years.
- **Architecture:** Dutch neoclassical and Art Deco buildings with red clay-tile roofs, clock towers, arcaded colonnades, tall shuttered windows; Chinese shophouses (ruko) with wooden shutters, upper balconies and recessed entrances; kampung houses of bamboo and wood with thatch or tile.
- **Harbor:** steamships with tall funnels, wooden sailing boats (prahu, pinisi), cargo cranes, stacked sacks and crates, warehouse (godown) fronts, wooden piers.
- **Clothing:** laborers in loose shirts, shorts or rolled trousers, bare feet, bamboo hats (caping); Javanese men in sarong, batik, blangkon; peci hats; women in kebaya and batik wraps; Peranakan Chinese in loose cotton jackets, trousers, kebaya encim; Dutch and Europeans in white linen suits, tropical helmets, tweed jackets and bow ties; officials with starched collars.
- **Light and mood:** hazy tropical light, warm dust, teal sky with cumulus.
- **Avoid:** modern cars, LED or neon signs, plastic goods, concrete high-rises, tarmac with painted lane markings, smartphones, modern logos.

## 2. Japanese occupation (1942–1945)

- **Mood:** scarcity and fear; palette darker, more desaturated, cold teal shadows, closed shutters.
- **Visuals:** queues for rice and cloth, sacks stamped with stencils, clothes made from coarse jute sacking, bicycles replacing cars, trucks with charcoal-gas generators at the back, blacked-out windows, boarded shops, stacked ration tokens.
- **Soldiers:** khaki uniforms, peaked caps with neck flaps, puttees; keep them in the background or as silhouettes, calm posture, no violence depicted.
- **Labor:** long lines of thin workers carrying tools or sacks, shown with dignity; no graphic suffering.
- **Avoid:** readable propaganda slogans unless supplied, caricature, gore, modern military hardware.

## 3. Revolution (1945–1949)

- **Visuals:** young men with headbands and old rifles, red-and-white flags on bamboo poles, barricades of crates and barrels, refugees with bundles on carts and trains, shops half-shut, black-market stalls, damaged shop fronts, armed patrols.
- **Mood:** urgent, fragile; golden dust, smoke on the horizon.
- **Handling:** non-graphic; emphasize resilience, families carrying goods, secret trade, makeshift kitchens.
- **Avoid:** gore, hate symbols, caricatured enemies.

## 4. Early independence (1950s–1960s)

- **Visuals:** old Dutch buildings with new signs, jeeps and small buses (oplet), early Vespa scooters, stacked cash bundles for inflation, import-license paperwork on desks, small workshops turning into factories, radios, typewriters, black-and-white framed leaders replaced by unreadable posters.
- **Clothing:** short-sleeved shirts, high-waisted trousers, peci, batik for ceremonies, women in kebaya and later simple dresses.
- **Mood:** hopeful and tense, warmer light returning.

## 5. Growth years and modern scale

- **1970s–1990s:** concrete factories, conveyor lines, delivery trucks, offices with typewriters and early computers, tall glass towers appearing, bigger signboards.
- **Modern scale shots:** container ports, cranes, container ships on blue open sea, aerial views of industrial estates, polished steel machinery.
- Brighter blues and reds, cleaner materials; the grain and haze remain.

## 6. Business props by trade

- **Coffee, spice, tea:** roasting drum, burlap sacks, wicker baskets, glass jars, paper bags with a simple emblem, scale and weights, grinding mill.
- **Textiles:** looms, bolts of batik, sewing machines, ledgers, shopfront with cloth rolls.
- **Trading:** abacus (sempoa), cash box, brass scales, ledgers, rubber stamps, bills of lading, telegrams.
- **Food and noodles:** steaming kettles, woks, wooden carts, bowls.
- **Transport:** bicycles with baskets, ox carts, trucks, steamships, prahu.
- **Banking:** brass grille counters, wooden desks, ledgers, ink pens, tall ceiling fans.

## 7. Sign text rules

- Put exact text in quotes and describe the lettering: "a dark blue enamel signboard with white serif capitals reading 'NV CERES'".
- Prefer period forms: "NV", "Toko", "Handel Maatschappij", "Warung", "Bank", "Kantor".
- Keep it to 1–3 words per sign; Nano Banana renders short text best.
- If the user gave no name, leave the sign blank or blurred, or invent a plainly fictional name.
- Do not add real current logos or trademarked packaging.

## 8. Scene-setting sentences to paste

- **Colonial street:** "A 1930s Dutch East Indies street of wet cobblestones, shuttered shophouses with wooden balconies, a red-tiled neoclassical building and a distant clock tower, coconut palms and a flamboyant tree at the roadside, no modern signage."
- **Pecinan shophouse:** "A 1930s Chinatown street in a Javanese port city: narrow two-story shophouses with carved wooden shutters, red paper lanterns, sacks of rice by the wall, a becak waiting at the corner, a coconut palm at the street end."
- **Harbor:** "A busy colonial Indonesian harbor with weathered wooden piers, stacked sacks, masted pinisi schooners and a steamship with tall funnels behind, coconut palms along the shore, no modern containers."
- **Interior workshop:** "A narrow workshop with a bare filament bulb, peeling teal-blue plaster, shelves of glass jars and burlap sacks, and a heavy steel machine."
- **Night market:** "A blue-hour night market with hanging paper lanterns, wooden stalls, steam from food carts, wet street reflections and orange bokeh."
- **Occupation:** "Japanese occupation era, 1943: a muted, quiet street with shuttered shops, a long queue of thin people holding cloth bags for rice, one soldier in khaki with a peaked cap in the far background."
- **Revolution:** "Late 1945: a barricade of crates and barrels across a dusty shophouse street, young men with headbands and a red-and-white flag, refugees pushing a handcart behind."
- **Independence:** "1950s: a dusty street with an old jeep, new hand-painted shop signs, a radio shop and men in short-sleeved shirts."
- **Empire scale:** "A modern container port at sunrise, a huge red container ship on a clean blue ocean with stacked multicolored containers."
