# Story beats to shots

BerakitKeHulu stories follow a rise-through-struggle arc. Use this to turn a narration script into a shot list with era, life stage, light and shot types.

## 2. Mapping script sentences to shots

- One concrete noun or action per image: choose the most filmable element of each sentence ("he carried sacks until his hands bled" → hands and burlap close-up, not an abstract "struggle").
- When a sentence names a place or object, show it; when it names an emotion, show the face; when it states a number or milestone, show a prop (price tag, banknotes, ledger).
- For a statement about hardship, choose a wide shot with a small figure; for a decision, choose a close-up on eyes or hands.
- When the script names a real person, translate to an invented archetype of the same role and age; leave the name to the voice-over.

## 3. Pacing and variety

- Alternate wide and close; never put three similar shots in a row.
- Keep the hero's description constant within an age.
- Insert an environment-only frame (street, harbor, sky) every 4–6 images for breathing room.
- Rising action gets progressively brighter, cleaner and bluer.

## 4. Transitions

- **Time-jump:** same street or landmark in two eras, same framing.
- **Match cut:** hands holding a sack → hands holding a ledger → hands on a steel control.
- **Season of life:** boy's face → young man's face with the same lighting direction.

## 5. Thumbnail frames

Use 16:9, a large expressive face on one third, a strong story object (roaster, ship, sacks) on another, and clean negative space in the third for title text. Choose an accent color from style-bible.md and keep the background blurred.
