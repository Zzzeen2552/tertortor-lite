# Eras and settings (Java, roughly 13th–17th century, as the Babad tells it)

Use for every prompt. Pick the era, add one scene-setting sentence, and avoid the anachronisms. Details are simplified for imagery and drawn from general sources; the Babad is a court chronicle with legend, so tell the user to verify specific claims against scholarly sources.

## 1. Mythic prelude and legend

- The chronicle opens with genealogy and cosmic origin, linking Javanese kings to Adam, the Pandawa and Hindu gods. Do not depict prophets or deities as figures. Show instead: a mountain in mist, a banyan tree, lontar leaves with unreadable script, a pool reflecting the sky, an empty stone seat.
- Legend scenes use the "mist grammar": heavy haze, low contrast, softly bled washes, few details.
- Legendary beings (such as the ruler of the southern sea, Nyai Roro Kidul) are shown respectfully as distant, veiled, ethereal presences, never as monsters or seductresses.

## 2. Majapahit (13th–early 16th century)

- **Core look:** plain red brick everywhere, carefully laid and eroded now. In the capital area around Trowulan: thick high brick walls, brick-based palace pavilions with carved wooden pillars and shingle or clay-ornamented roofs, ordinary homes with brick floors and thatch, wells, drains and canals, markets, goldsmiths, potters.
- **Gates:** split gates (candi bentar) of plain red brick, stepped and doorless, like Wringin Lawang (about 15 m tall); roofed gates (paduraksa) like Bajang Ratu with narrative reliefs on the top; brick walls with narrow passages.
- **Water:** large rectangular brick-walled reservoir (Segaran), sunken bathing basins with steps (like Tikus temple, with a central structure modeled on Mount Mahameru), canals around pavilions.
- **Landscape:** teak forest and rice fields on a flat plain, volcanoes (Penanggungan, Arjuno, Kelud) on the horizon, banyan trees at courtyards, coconut palms.
- **Religion:** Hindu-Buddhist; statues, small shrines, offering bowls, incense; show with respect.
- **Founding legend:** forest clearing, maja fruit tree (bitter fruit), men clearing land, first brick walls rising.
- **People:** courtiers in kain and shoulder cloths, craftspeople, farmers, traders using small coins, warriors with spear and shield.

## 3. Demak (early 16th century)

- **Mosque:** Great Mosque of Demak is a timber building with a multi-tiered pyramidal roof (no dome, no minaret), supported by four teak pillars (saka guru), with carved wooden doors. Show it from a low viewpoint across an ablution pool or courtyard; tiered form echoes the meru of earlier Hindu-Buddhist Java.
- **Port and town:** a coastal trading town on a river and old shoreline, boatbuilding yards, wooden piers, warehouses, markets; palm and mangrove coast.
- **People:** a mix of Javanese, Arab, Gujarati, Chinese and Malay traders; teachers in turbans and long robes (shown from behind or at distance), students sitting in pavilions, villagers in kain and simple tunics.
- **Mood:** continuity and change; old brick gates beside timber mosque.
- **Care:** Wali and religious teachers appear only from behind, at a distance or in shadow, never with faces and never named.

## 4. Pajang (second half of 16th century)

- Rural-court setting inland: a modest palace compound with brick walls, a pendopo and a large open square (alun-alun), banyan trees, rice fields and the broad Bengawan river with bamboo rafts.
- Legend and politics: a young man's rise from the countryside to the court; a feud between rival houses; ritual weapons (keris, spear).
- Keep battles distant and silhouetted; show aftermath, banners and quiet.

## 5. Mataram (late 16th–17th century)

- **Kotagede:** the early capital with brick walls, gates and bastions, banyan courts, a royal burial ground on a hill, a market (pasar), cobbled lanes, houses of wood and brick with thatch or tile.
- **Kraton form:** a walled compound with an open alun-alun, two banyan trees in the square, gates in series, a joglo pendopo with a tiered ceiling, carved teak pillars, mat floors, gamelan sets.
- **Forest founding:** the land of Mentaok cleared by a small group of men and women; fires, huts, hand tools.
- **Sultan Agung era:** large armies on the move, banners and spears; a long road with ox carts; the siege of Batavia can appear as distant fortress walls and a small figure line; avoid triumphal framing and violence.
- **South sea:** Parangkusumo-style beach with dark sand, surf and mist for legend scenes.

## 6. Clothing

Keep clothing simple, drawn loosely.

**Men**
- Majapahit: kain wrapped and tucked at the waist, a shoulder cloth (sampur or selendang), a headcloth tied simply (iket or udeng), a keris, simple ornaments; warriors with tunic or wrapped cloth and a shield.
- Demak onward: kain, a long upper garment or tunic, a turban or iket, a sash; keris tucked at the back of the waist in court dress; teachers in long robes and white turbans.
- Mataram court: kain with batik of the era (parang, kawung), a fitted jacket (surjan style), a headcloth tied in the iket style; the pre-shaped blangkon is a later invention, avoid before the 18th century.

**Women**
- Majapahit: kain wrapped with a kemben or a short fitted bodice, a shoulder cloth, hair in a bun with a simple ornament.
- Demak and Mataram: kain and kemben with a fitted jacket or early kebaya-like blouse, a bun with a flower, a shoulder cloth.

**Everyone**
- Fabrics are woven or batik in earth tones, indigo, soga brown, saffron, madder red; borders and checks rather than bright patterns.
- Common people in simpler wraps; the poor barefoot; rulers with gold ornaments drawn as small accent touches.

## 7. Props and craft

- Lontar leaves with a stylus and a small knife for writing; palm-oil or coconut-oil lamps; clay jars and burnished water ewers; woven bamboo baskets; banana-leaf wrapping; wooden carts with oxen; buffalo; horses for nobles.
- Gamelan bronze instruments, drums, wayang puppets of leather.
- Keris, spear (tombak), shield, bow; flags and umbrellas of rank.
- Wooden boats, bamboo rafts, outrigger boats on the north coast.
- Offering bowls with flowers, incense in small holders.

## 8. Anachronisms to avoid

Domes and minarets in Demak or earlier; marble floors; glass lamps and windows; European uniforms, ships or guns before the arrival of Europeans, and no European architecture in Java before the colonial period; printed books; pre-shaped blangkon; kebaya in modern cut; synthetic bright batik; concrete, electric light, metal roofs; pavement; Balinese-specific ornament for Javanese scenes; Chinese-style roofs on mosques unless the story calls for it.

## 9. Scene-setting sentences to paste

- **Majapahit gate:** "A 14th-century Majapahit split gate of plain red brick, stepped and doorless, with moss on its worn steps, a banyan tree and a coconut palm behind it on a flat plain, a faint volcano silhouette on the horizon."
- **Majapahit pool:** "A brick-walled royal reservoir with stepped sides, a small pavilion on a brick base, and a woman in kain and shoulder cloth filling a clay jar at the edge."
- **Trowulan street:** "A lane between thick brick walls, a thatched house with a brick floor and a clay water jar by the door, a potter's wheel under a lean-to."
- **Demak mosque:** "The timber Great Mosque of Demak with a tiered pyramidal roof, no dome and no minaret, carved wooden doors and a still ablution pool in the foreground."
- **Demak port:** "A north-coast trading port with wooden piers, outrigger boats, warehouses of wood and thatch, mangroves and palms, traders of several peoples in simple clothing."
- **Pajang river:** "A broad river with bamboo rafts below a modest brick-walled court, an open square with two banyan trees and farmland beyond."
- **Mataram forest:** "A forest clearing near a river, small thatched huts, smoke rising, men with axes and women with baskets, a pile of new red bricks beside a half-built wall."
- **Kotagede:** "Early Mataram: a brick-walled compound with a gate in series, a banyan court, a joglo pendopo with carved teak pillars, market sellers sitting on mats."
- **Court audience:** "A wooden pendopo with carved pillars and mat floor, a ruler seated on a low dais, courtiers kneeling in rows seen from behind, gamelan instruments at the side."
- **South sea legend:** "A dark-sand beach with long surf and thick mist, a distant veiled female figure in green-toned cloth at the water's edge, a cliff fading into paper tone."
- **Lontar scribe:** "Palm-leaf manuscripts spread on a low wooden table, a stylus in an inked hand, an oil lamp, a brick wall behind."
